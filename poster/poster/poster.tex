\rule{0pt}{0pt}%
\begin{pbox}{vodark}{volight}{Why chemical identity breaks down}
  \justifying
  In chemistry, identity is exact: \textbf{InChI\,/\,InChIKey} link a molecule
  unambiguously across PubChem and ChEBI.\\[4mm]
  In EU regulation (\textbf{REACH}, \textbf{CLP}) a substance may be a molecule,
  a mixture or \textbf{UVCB}, a \textbf{group} such as PFAS with no set
  membership, or an analytical sum parameter --- carrying only \textbf{CAS
  numbers} and names, which encode no structure.
\end{pbox}

\begin{minipage}[t][\colh][s]{\linewidth}
\rule{0pt}{0pt}%
\begin{pbox}{vodark}{volight}{What the data shows}
  \justifying
  \textbf{Linkability (RQ1).} Of 33,452 unique entries, \textbf{62.5\%
  (20,909)} link directly via InChIKey. The other \textbf{37.5\%} resist
  structure-based linking: 11.6\% unresolved CAS (tier 4), 10.8\% mixtures or
  UVCBs (tier 5), 14.9\% no usable identifier (tier 7).\\[5mm]
  \textbf{CAS reliability (RQ2).} \textbf{70.0\%} of structure-resolved
  InChIKeys occur in a single source, so CAS cannot serve as a shared key.
  Ambiguity compounds this: 195 InChIKeys carry multiple CAS numbers (up to
  seven) and 4 CAS numbers resolve to two distinct InChIKeys --- for example
  toluene diisocyanate, which maps to two positional isomers.
\end{pbox}

\vfill
\begin{pbox}{vodark}{volight}{Embeddings \& LLMs cannot fill the gap}
  \justifying
  \textbf{Embeddings \& LLM (RQ3).} Embedding-based matching collapses: the best model reaches \textbf{Hit@1 =
  5.75\%}, the rest score below 1\%. Claude Sonnet 4.6 reaches \textbf{39.2\%}
  --- a seven-fold gain, yet it still misclassifies the majority. Text is not a
  substitute for structure.
  
\end{pbox}

\begin{column}{0.315\textwidth}
\begin{minipage}[t][\colh][s]{\linewidth}
\rule{0pt}{0pt}%
\begin{pbox}{vodark}{volight}{What the model makes possible}
  \justifying
  \textbf{Two axes, not one.} ChemOnt classifies substances chemically
  (\texttt{skos:broader}); ChEBI says what they \emph{do}. Both reach the same
  concept, so ``every substance on an obligation list acting as a carcinogenic
  agent'' is one query.\\[4mm]
  \textbf{Provenance survives integration.} \textbf{48,438
  \texttt{oa:Annotation}} nodes carry the cross-domain links (40,644 list
  memberships, 7,794 ChEBI roles) so 18 sources can disagree without
  overwriting one another.\\[4mm]
  \textbf{A classification, not just a tree.} Four
  \texttt{xkos:ClassificationLevel} steps (kingdom $\rightarrow$ subclass) make
  ChemOnt navigable at a chosen granularity.
\end{pbox}

\vfill
\begin{pbox}{vodark}{volight}{Scope mapping, kept separate}
  \justifying
  For entries without a structure, an LLM assesses how a regulatory group
  relates to a ChemOnt node, yielding \textbf{304 validated SKOS triples}
  (285 \texttt{broadMatch}, 18 \texttt{exactMatch}, 1 \texttt{narrowMatch}).
  Regulatory groups are constrained \emph{subsets} of a ChemOnt class, not
  equivalents.\\[5mm]
  It is published as a \textbf{separate graph}: machine-generated mappings do
  not enter the core until validated by experts.
\end{pbox}

\vfill
\begin{pbox}{vodark}{volight}{Conclusions \& recommendations}
  \justifying
  Over one-third of entries cannot be linked to a structure, CAS adds ambiguity,
  and neither embeddings nor LLMs replace structural information. Manual
  reconciliation (${\sim}$3,150 person-years for REACH) is infeasible.
  \textbf{Harmonisation must happen at the source.}\\[4mm]
  \textbf{1}\enspace Require structure-based identifiers (InChIKey; MInChI for
  mixtures) across instruments.\\[3mm]
  \textbf{2}\enspace Mandate explicit substance-group representation via
  ontologies such as ChemOnt.\\[3mm]
  \textbf{3}\enspace Publish regulatory data as linked data with persistent URIs
  and SKOS links.
\end{pbox}
